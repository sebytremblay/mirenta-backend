Hiring, training, and retaining at scale

The 10-K ties comparable sales and expansion success to attracting and retaining qualified Team Members, especially store managers. Roughly 100 new stores a year on a 2,395-store base toward a 3,000-store goal makes frontline staffing and store-leadership development a recurring operational pressure.

Rising labor and healthcare costs

The 10-K names higher labor and healthcare costs as a margin risk. With the FY2026 operating margin outlook at 9.3 to 9.6 percent, below the 10 percent-plus long-term goal, supporting associates without proportional headcount growth becomes essential.

Dependence on information systems and security

Managing expansion depends on the adequacy of existing information systems. As store-facing AI and computer vision roll out across thousands of locations, supporting and securing that footprint becomes a growing demand, and outages or access issues stop revenue and shipments directly.

Tariff, supply chain, and transportation cost

Q4 FY2025 gross margin felt pressure from higher tariffs and transportation costs, with imports at 11.4 percent of total sales for the year. These dynamics squeeze the operating model and intensify the need to do more with existing resources.

Softening discretionary demand

CEO Hal Lawton noted Q4 came in below expectations as discretionary demand moderated while essentials stayed resilient. The 1 to 3 percent comparable sales outlook reflects a cautious consumer, raising the bar for disciplined support functions.

A deskless majority waiting in a queue

Half the team works part-time and most never sit at a desk. They need answers on pay, PTO balances, benefits deadlines, locked accounts, or a down POS lane while standing in a store aisle or on a DC dock, not while waiting in a corporate ticket queue.

The point of view

Supporting both populations, the way Hey GURA already supports the floor

Tractor Supply runs on its people. More than 52,000 Team Members across 2,602 stores, 10 distribution centers, and the Brentwood Store Support Center carry the Life Out Here promise to customers every day. The 2030 strategy raises the bar: 6 to 8 percent annual net sales growth, roughly 100 new stores a year toward a 3,000-store target, and a path back toward a 10 percent operating margin. Every one of those goals depends on hiring, onboarding, and keeping qualified Team Members, and on running the technology that stores and distribution centers cannot operate without. For the Employee Experience function you lead, this is the gap between the experience Tractor Supply gives customers and the one it gives its own Team Members.

The workforce shape makes that hard in a specific way. Half the team works part-time, turnover runs high in hourly retail, and most Team Members never sit at a desk. They need an answer about a pay question, a PTO balance, a benefits deadline, a locked account, or a down POS lane while standing in a store aisle or a DC dock, not while waiting in a ticket queue. Seasonal hiring surges ahead of spring and the fourth quarter pile onboarding, account provisioning, and equipment requests onto the same support teams at the same time. Rising labor and healthcare costs, tariff pressure, and a cautious consumer leave little room to add headcount to keep up. The work grows; the budget to staff it does not.

Agentforce for Employee Service addresses both populations directly. HR Service and IT Service Management put autonomous AI agents inside Slack, mobile, and a portal, where Team Members already work, so they resolve routine HR and IT questions in conversation instead of filing tickets. The platform grounds answers in your own policies and systems and hands off to a person the moment a request turns sensitive or complex. The point is not to replace your HR and IT teams. The point is to free them to spend their hours on the work that needs human judgment, the same way Hey GURA already frees store associates to serve customers.
