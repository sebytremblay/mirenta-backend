What the reposition changed

The pre-reposition baseline sold to a revenue organization through the sales_marketing vertical, whose value model multiplies a full rep roster by full baseline meetings and overstates ROI. The reposition adds a founder_led_sales vertical that measures what a founder actually guards and speaks founder to founder.

Before: enterprise framing

  • Targets a revenue org, AEs, and a rep roster.
  • Headline is a runaway ROI percent.
  • Value math multiplies full headcount by full baseline meetings.
  • Five-year cumulative projection chart.
  • Hero leads with the seller value claim.

After: founder-led framing

  • Targets the founder and whoever runs growth beside them.
  • Headline is a bounded multiple (for example 4.3x).
  • Value math counts deals won from earned replies plus founder hours returned, at a named hourly rate.
  • No projection chart; a founder guards the near term.
  • Hero mirrors the lead's own reality first, then introduces the seller as a peer.

The blocker the adversarial panel caught

The iteration-4 page passed the two original reviewers at 8.25 / 8.15 with zero blockers. A final verification step ran a fresh, diverse three-reviewer panel over the same committed page. All three flagged the same defect:

Verification reviewerThe hero says "Fifty design partners shipped" but Cedar has 40 design partners out of YC W25. A wrong headline number about my own company, in line two, is the tell that this is automation posing as hand research.

Why the page validator did not catch it: the validator (D54) enforces every computed figure verbatim, not researched prose numbers. "40 design partners" is prose, so the cheap Haiku builder felt free to paraphrase it to "Fifty." The arithmetic reconciled for all three reviewers, so the math fix from iteration 3 held; the failure was fidelity to a researched fact, not internal consistency.

The source fix, and why iteration 5 still falls short

Fixed at the source, not cosmetically. Two changes, both structural:

  1. A vertical-neutral rule in the byte-stable DESIGN_BRIEF (D53): every researched prose number (count, date, round size, batch name) carries the same verbatim rigor as a computed dollar figure. The brief stays cache-stable and vertical-neutral; the fidelity rule is general.
  2. A deterministic render gate inside the workflow: it rejects any "design partner" count other than 40, any em or en dash, and any missing computed figure, and retries the render until clean, so a stochastic model slip never reaches the human panel.

The committed iteration-5 page states "40 design partners" twice, carries zero dashes, renders every computed figure verbatim, and greps clean against the G2 blocklist.

Why the loop stopped here instead of re-rolling

The gate caps iterations at five "else log BLOCKED." Iteration 4 scored high but lied about the lead's own company; iteration 5 is true but scores below the bar. Re-rolling the Haiku builder a sixth time, hoping it keeps the count and names the specifics, is the reward-hack the cap exists to forbid. Shipping a page that misstates the lead's own design-partner count is strictly worse than shipping one that is accurate but less dazzling, so the committed artifact is iteration 5.

Logged human question: the founder-led page cannot, with the Haiku builder pinned by D47 for cost, state the lead's count correctly and score both original reviewers at or above 8.0 within five iterations. Two paths, both a quality-versus-cost call for a human:

  1. Add a per-render required-facts instruction that names the must-cite specifics (Cedar Cash, Basis Set, the seed size, the W25 batch) and guards the domain-inversion line, then re-open the gate.
  2. Raise the builder model above the Haiku tier for this segment, which reopens D47 (cost).
